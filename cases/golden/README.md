# Golden Case Patterns

Use these as abstract reasoning references.

## Hard MOQ vs Trial Order
Customer wants a small test quantity below confirmed production MOQ. Do not promise an exception. Explain the production constraint clearly, acknowledge the customer's inventory risk, and look for commercially realistic alternatives.

## Annual Volume vs Per-Order Basis
Customer references large annual volume, while the quoted economics depend on quantity per shipment/order. Clarify the pricing basis before discussing concessions.

## Internal Data vs Formal Report
Supplier has internal technical data but lacks the formal third-party/regulatory document requested. Be transparent about current evidence, avoid presenting it as equivalent, and propose a staged route to formal validation.

## Stalled Near Decision
A mature project goes quiet near the final decision. Follow up with genuine project concern and a low-pressure reopening path rather than a generic “just following up”.

## Existing Customer Natural Tone
Long-term customers do not need stiff customer-service language. Preserve professionalism while sounding familiar, cooperative, and efficient.

## IP-Sensitive Reference Design
When a requested 1:1 design may involve third-party IP, do not casually promise replication. Offer structurally similar but safer alternatives or ask for authorization.
